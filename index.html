Torque (Newton-Meters / Nm)

Torque measures rotational twisting power. High torque (70–95 Nm) is what launches you from red lights and lets you pedal up 20% hills without stalling. If you live in San Francisco, Seattle, or haul kids, torque matters far more than watts.

Motor Power (Watts / W)

Watts quantify the rate of electrical energy output. Continuous watts (typically 250W–750W) govern sustained top cruising speeds on flat ground. Note: peak watts can momentarily reach 1.5x continuous rating during hard acceleration.

Watt-Hour (Wh) Capacity Calculator

Watt-Hours (Wh) represent your battery's actual gas tank size. Formula: Voltage (V) × Amp-Hours (Ah) = Watt-Hours (Wh).

Real-World Environmental Range Modifiers

Freezing Temperatures

Below 40°F (5°C), lithium chemistry slows down, causing a temporary 20% to 30% reduction in total range.

Elevation & Climbing

Sustained 10%+ uphill riding demands 3.5x more continuous motor current than level pavement cruising.

Headwinds & Speed

Aerodynamic drag squares with speed. Cruising at 28 mph consumes nearly twice the Wh per mile as 18 mph.

Low Tire Pressure

Under-inflated tires dramatically increase rolling resistance. Maintaining rated PSI preserves 5–8 miles of range.

Heavy Rider & Cargo

Every additional 30 lbs of passenger or cargo weight reduces range by approximately 5% on hilly routes.

Pedal Cadence & Gearing

Spinning pedals at 70–85 RPM allows mid-drive motors to run at their thermal and electrical peak efficiency.

Mandatory Battery Charging Safety & Regulatory Compliance

Always use the manufacturer-certified charger with correct voltage shutoff thresholds. Never charge an e-bike battery unattended overnight or in stairwells blocking primary residential exit doors. If a pack is dropped or displays swelling, retire it immediately.

E-Bike Legal Classes: Class 1 (pedal-assist only, 20 mph max), Class 2 (throttle + assist, 20 mph max), Class 3 (pedal-assist only, 28 mph max, speedometer required). Legal definitions and trail permissions vary by country, state, and city. Always check your local jurisdiction laws.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything first-time e-bike buyers and experienced commuters need to know before investing.

Manufacturers benchmark range on flat indoor roll tracks with a 140 lb rider, zero wind resistance, warm 72°F temperatures, and in the lowest possible Eco assist mode (which provides only 15–20% motor assist). In real urban commuting with stoplights, hills, cold weather, and brisk acceleration, expect 55% to 75% of advertised maximum claims. Our Range Matcher uses real road telemetry to give realistic figures.
Class 1: Pedal-assist only (no throttle), motor cuts out at 20 mph. These bikes enjoy the broadest legal access to multi-use paved paths and municipal bike trails.
Class 2: Equipped with a throttle (push button or twist grip) that propels the bike up to 20 mph without pedaling. Great for intersection starts, but restricted on some natural surface trails.
Class 3: Pedal-assist up to 28 mph. Requires a speedometer. Ideal for fast road commuting alongside motor vehicle traffic, but often restricted on pedestrian shared paths.
Choose a Hub Motor if you ride mostly flat to gently rolling city streets, want zero chain wear, prefer budget-friendly replacement costs ($1,200–$1,900), and like having a throttle option. Choose a Mid-Drive if you tackle steep hills, haul heavy cargo or kids, or want an exceptionally natural acoustic bike pedaling feel through your chain gears.
Never charge a frozen battery pack. Bring the battery indoors and allow it to reach room temperature (around 65°F / 18°C) before plugging in the charger. If storing your bike for weeks during heavy snow, store the pack at approximately 50% to 70% state of charge in a dry, temperate room away from flammable materials.
